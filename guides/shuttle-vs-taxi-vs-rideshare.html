Landing is the easy part. The real decision starts at baggage claim: queue for a taxi, open a rideshare app, find the shuttle desk, or walk straight to a driver holding your name? Here is how the options really compare.

The four main options

  • Shared shuttle — you pay per seat and share a van with other passengers. Cheapest for one or two people, but expect several hotel stops.
  • Taxi — no booking needed, metered or flat-rate. Price certainty varies by city; queues at peak times can exceed 30 minutes.
  • Rideshare — convenient in cities where it is allowed at the airport, but surge pricing after big arrival waves can double fares.
  • Private transfer — a pre-booked car or van at a fixed price, with meet & greet and flight tracking.

The group break-even rule

Shared shuttles charge per person; private transfers charge per vehicle. That means the math flips quickly. If a shared seat costs $25 and a private sedan costs $85, the break-even is 3.4 passengers — so a family of four almost always does better with a private car, and gets there faster.

Rule of thumb: 1–2 travellers → shared shuttle or train. 3+ travellers, kids, or lots of luggage → private transfer or van.

Hidden costs to watch

  • Tolls and airport access fees (often excluded from walk-up fares).
  • Late-night surcharges on taxis.
  • Surge pricing on rideshare apps.
  • Waiting-time charges if your flight is delayed and the booking has no flight tracking.

When a pre-booked transfer is clearly worth it

Late-night arrivals, first visits to a city, travelling with children who need car seats, and trips where the destination is far from the city centre (resorts, ski areas, cruise ports) are the situations where a fixed-price transfer wins on both price and stress.
